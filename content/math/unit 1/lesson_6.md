## 3. Lesson Content

**Unit:** Unit 1 | **Lesson:** Lesson 6 | **Topic:** Comparing numbers in multiple forms

### Mathematical Concepts

* You can compare numbers in any form: standard form, expanded form and word form.
* You may **convert to standard form** to compare, **or** use place value in expanded form or in word form to compare.
* The comparison itself follows the same rules as comparing big numbers: the number with more digits is greater, and numbers with equally many digits are compared from the left until the digits differ.

### Definitions

**Definition — Multiple forms of a number**

The multiple forms of a number are the standard form (digits with commas), the expanded form (a sum of place values), the word form (words with commas between the periods), the short-word form (digits plus period names), and the product form, (digit × place value) sums.

### Rules

**Rule — Comparing in multiple forms**

Numbers may be compared in any form. The safest strategy is to convert every number into standard form first, then compare digit by digit from the left.

**Rule — Using place value directly**

Instead of converting, the comparison can be made by looking at the greatest place value present in each number, because the number with the greater value in the highest differing place is the greater number.

### Procedures / Methods

**Method: Comparing two numbers written in different forms**

1. Read each number carefully and note which form it is written in.
2. Convert each number into standard form:
   * from word or short-word form, write each period with three digits;
   * from expanded form, add the values;
   * from product form, work out each product and add.
3. Compare the two standard-form numbers: count digits first, then compare from the left.
4. Write the correct symbol: >, < or =.

### Mathematical Relationships

* Different forms of the same number are equal, so a comparison between two forms is really a comparison between the two numbers they represent.
* Expanded form shows place values directly, which makes it easy to spot the greatest place value in each number.

### Examples

#### Example 3 (source)

**Problem:** Write (>, < or =) to compare.
a. 70,000 + 4,000 + 50 + 7 ___ 70,000 + 4,000 + 500 + 70
b. Two milliard, seven hundred thirty-eight thousand, ten ___ Two milliard, seven hundred thirty-five thousand, eleven
c. 3,000,000 + 7,000 + 800 + 9 ___ Three million, seven thousand, eight hundred nine
d. (7 × 1,000,000) + (5 × 100,000) + (3 × 1,000) + (4 × 100) + (9 × 1) ___ 7,000,000 + 500,000 + 3,000 + 400 + 90

**Solution (converting to standard form):**
a. 74,057 and 74,570 → 74,057 < 74,570
b. 2,000,738,010 and 2,000,735,011 → 2,000,738,010 > 2,000,735,011
c. 3,007,809 and 3,007,809 → equal
d. 7,503,409 and 7,503,490 → 7,503,409 < 7,503,490

**Final Answer:** a. < b. > c. = d. <

## 4. Key Mathematical Vocabulary

### Standard form

**Meaning:** The number written in digits with commas separating the periods.
**Example:** 74,057

### Expanded form

**Meaning:** The number written as a sum of the values of its digits.
**Example:** 70,000 + 4,000 + 50 + 7 = 74,057

### Word form

**Meaning:** The number written in words with commas between the periods.
**Example:** Two milliard, seven hundred thirty-eight thousand, ten = 2,000,738,010

### Product form (digit × place value)

**Meaning:** The number written as a sum of each digit multiplied by the value of its place.
**Example:** (7 × 1,000,000) + (5 × 100,000) + (3 × 1,000) + (4 × 100) + (9 × 1) = 7,503,409

### Convert

**Meaning:** To rewrite a number from one form into another, usually into standard form so that it can be compared.
**Example:** "7 ten thousands" converts to 70,000.

## 5. Simplified Student Explanation

**Unit:** Unit 1 | **Lesson:** Lesson 6 | **Topic:** Comparing Numbers in Multiple Forms

Sometimes the two numbers you must compare are written in different ways. One may be in words and the other in digits, or one may be an expanded form.

The easiest plan is: **turn both numbers into standard form first**, then compare as usual (count the digits, then compare from the left).

Example: 70,000 + 4,000 + 50 + 7 ___ 70,000 + 4,000 + 500 + 70
* First number = 74,057
* Second number = 74,570
* Compare from the left: 74 is the same, then 0 < 5 in the Hundreds place.
* So 74,057 < 74,570.

Example: "Two milliard, seven hundred thirty-eight thousand, ten" ___ "Two milliard, seven hundred thirty-five thousand, eleven"
* First = 2,000,738,010, second = 2,000,735,011
* Compare from the left: the difference appears in the Thousands place: 8 > 5.
* So the first is greater.

Careful with units written in words:
* 400 thousands = 400,000
* 7 ten thousands = 70,000
* 7,000 millions = 7,000,000,000 = 7 milliards (so these two are equal!)

## 6. Important Rules and Formulas

### Rule 1 — Any form can be compared

Numbers can be compared in standard, expanded, word or short-word form.

### Rule 2 — Best strategy

Convert both numbers to standard form, then compare digits from the left; or use place value directly in the expanded or word form.

### Formula 1 — Useful conversions

* `n thousands = n × 1,000`
* `n hundreds = n × 100`
* `n tens = n × 10`
* `n millions = n × 1,000,000`
* `n milliards = n × 1,000,000,000`
Example: 7,000 millions = 7,000 × 1,000,000 = 7,000,000,000 = 7 milliards.

### Important Note

Two numbers written in different forms can still be exactly equal; always finish the conversion before choosing the symbol.

## 8. Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Expanded form against expanded form

**Problem:** Compare 70,000 + 4,000 + 50 + 7 and 70,000 + 4,000 + 500 + 70.

**Method (textbook method):** Convert both to standard form, then compare from the left.

**Solution:**
Step 1: First number = 74,057.
Step 2: Second number = 74,570.
Step 3: The Ten Thousands and Thousands digits are the same (74), then the Hundreds place gives 0 < 5.

**Final Answer:** 74,057 < 74,570

### Example 2 — Word form against word form

**Problem:** Compare "Two milliard, seven hundred thirty-eight thousand, ten" and "Two milliard, seven hundred thirty-five thousand, eleven".

**Method:** Convert both to standard form using three digits per period.

**Solution:**
Step 1: First = 2 | 000 | 738 | 010 = 2,000,738,010.
Step 2: Second = 2 | 000 | 735 | 011 = 2,000,735,011.
Step 3: Compare from the left; the first difference is in the Thousands place: 8 > 5.

**Final Answer:** 2,000,738,010 > 2,000,735,011

### Example 3 — Product form against expanded form

**Problem:** Compare (7 × 1,000,000) + (5 × 100,000) + (3 × 1,000) + (4 × 100) + (9 × 1) and 7,000,000 + 500,000 + 3,000 + 400 + 90.

**Method:** Work out the products, add, then compare.

**Solution:**
Step 1: First = 7,000,000 + 500,000 + 3,000 + 400 + 9 = 7,503,409.
Step 2: Second = 7,000,000 + 500,000 + 3,000 + 400 + 90 = 7,503,490.
Step 3: Compare from the left; the Tens place gives 0 < 9.

**Final Answer:** 7,503,409 < 7,503,490

## 9. Common Mistakes

### Common Mistake 1

**Mistake:** Comparing the two forms without converting, for example thinking that a number written in words must be bigger because it looks longer.

**Correct Method:** Convert both numbers to standard form and then compare, as the source suggests.

**Why:** The source's note for parents states that the child may struggle with comparing numbers in word form or expanded form and may convert to standard form to compare.

### Common Mistake 2

**Mistake:** Misreading units such as "7,000 millions" or "400 thousands".

**Correct Method:** Multiply by the value of the unit: 7,000 millions = 7,000,000,000; 400 thousands = 400,000.

**Why:** Each unit (thousand, million, milliard) has a fixed value, so the number of units must be multiplied by it before comparing.
