## 1. Lesson Overview

**Unit:** Unit 1 | **Lesson:** Lesson 7 | **Topic:** Ordering numbers in ascending and descending order

**Lesson title:** Descending and Ascending Numbers

**Main mathematical topic:** Ordering a set of whole numbers from least to greatest (ascending order) and from greatest to least (descending order), including numbers given in different forms.

**What students learn in Lesson 7:**

* Ascending order means ordering numbers from least to greatest.
* Descending order means ordering numbers from greatest to least.
* A number with more digits is the greatest in the set; a number with fewer digits is the least.
* Numbers with the same number of digits are ordered by comparing places from the left.
* Real data (such as the population of governorates) can be ordered using these rules.
* Numbers given in word, expanded or product form should first be written in standard form, then ordered.

**Main mathematical concepts:** ascending order, descending order, comparing by number of digits, comparing place by place from the left, ordering numbers in multiple forms.

**Important skills developed:** ordering numbers in multiple forms; describing strategies for ordering numbers; aligning numbers to compare digits from left to right.

## 3. Lesson Content

**Unit:** Unit 1 | **Lesson:** Lesson 7 | **Topic:** Ordering numbers

### Mathematical Concepts

#### Ordering numbers using the population table

The source table shows the population of four governorates in Egypt in 2021:

| Governorate | Population |
|---|---|
| Cairo | 10,058,942 |
| North Sinai | 450,528 |
| Alexandria | 5,452,718 |
| Souhag | 5,510,876 |

The governorates are ordered by population from greatest to least as follows:

1. The number 10,058,942 has the most digits, so it is the greatest number.
2. The number 450,528 has the least number of digits, so it is the least number.
3. Compare 5,452,718 and 5,510,876, which have the same number of digits. Check each place until the digits are different.

| Step 1 | Step 2 |
|---|---|
| Compare the Millions: 5,452,718 and 5,510,876 → same digit of Millions (5) | Compare the Hundred Thousands: 4 and 5 → 5 > 4 |

Then: 5,510,876 > 5,452,718.

**From above:** 10,058,942 > 5,510,876 > 5,452,718 > 450,528

In order of their population, the governorates are Cairo, Souhag, Alexandria and North Sinai.

### Definitions

**Definition — Ascending order**

Ascending order is ordering numbers from least to greatest, for example 1,188,580 → 2,517,550 → 2,896,016.

**Definition — Descending order**

Descending order is ordering numbers from greatest to least, for example 7,500,600,300 → 7,400,001,002 → 7,040,005,300 → 745,300.

### Rules

**Rule — The number of digits decides first**

In a set of whole numbers, the number with the most digits is the greatest and the number with the fewest digits is the least.

**Rule — Comparing numbers with equally many digits**

Start comparison at the greatest place value (the leftmost place) and move right until the digits are different.

**Rule — Ordering numbers given in multiple forms**

Write every number in standard form first, then order them.

### Procedures / Methods

**Method: Writing numbers in ascending order (textbook method)**

1. Write the numbers, lining up the places; determine the smallest number.
2. Write the remaining numbers, lining up the places, and compare them.
3. Write the numbers from least to greatest.

**Method: Writing numbers in descending order**

1. Write every number in standard form if it is given in another form.
2. Find the greatest number (most digits, or greatest digit in the highest place).
3. Continue choosing the greatest of the remaining numbers.
4. Write the list from greatest to least.

### Mathematical Relationships

* Ascending order is the reverse of descending order for the same set of numbers.
* Ordering is repeated comparison: each pair of neighbouring numbers in an ordered list satisfies a > or < relation.

### Examples

#### Example 1 (source)

**Problem:** Write these numbers in an ascending order: 2,896,016; 1,188,580; 2,517,550

**Solution (textbook steps):**
Step 1: Write the numbers, lining up places, and determine the smallest number → 1,188,580 is the smallest.
Step 2: Write the remaining numbers, lining up places, and compare → 2,517,550 is smaller than 2,896,016.
Step 3: Write the numbers from least to greatest.

**Final Answer:** 1,188,580 , 2,517,550 , 2,896,016

#### Example 2 (source)

**Problem:** Write each of the following numbers in standard form, then arrange them in a descending order.
* (7 × 1,000,000,000) + (4 × 10,000,000) + (5 × 1,000) + (3 × 100)
* Seven milliard, four hundred million, one thousand, two
* 7,000,000,000 + 500,000,000 + 600,000 + 300
* 745,300

**Solution (source table):**

| Standard form | Descendingly |
|---|---|
| 7,040,005,300 | 7,500,600,300 |
| 7,400,001,002 | 7,400,001,002 |
| 7,500,600,300 | 7,040,005,300 |
| 745,300 | 745,300 |

**Final Answer:** 7,500,600,300 > 7,400,001,002 > 7,040,005,300 > 745,300

## 5. Simplified Student Explanation

**Unit:** Unit 1 | **Lesson:** Lesson 7 | **Topic:** Descending and Ascending Numbers

**Ascending** = going up: from the smallest to the biggest.
**Descending** = going down: from the biggest to the smallest.

How to order a group of numbers:

1. If any number is written in words, in expanded form, or as products, change it to standard form first.
2. Count the digits. The number with the most digits is the biggest. The number with the fewest digits is the smallest.
3. If two numbers have the same number of digits, compare them from the left until the digits are different.
4. Write the list in the order you were asked for.

Example: order 2,896,016 , 1,188,580 , 2,517,550 in ascending order.
* 1,188,580 starts with 1, the others start with 2, so 1,188,580 is the smallest.
* Between 2,896,016 and 2,517,550: the Hundred Thousands place gives 8 > 5, so 2,517,550 is smaller.
* Ascending: 1,188,580 , 2,517,550 , 2,896,016.

A useful tip from the book: write the numbers under each other so the places line up, then compare digits from left to right.

## 6. Important Rules and Formulas

### Rule 1 — Ascending order

From least to greatest.

### Rule 2 — Descending order

From greatest to least.

### Rule 3 — Start with the number of digits

More digits → greater number; fewer digits → smaller number.

### Rule 4 — Same number of digits

Begin the comparison at the greatest place value and move right until the digits differ.

### Rule 5 — Multiple forms

Convert all numbers to standard form before ordering.

### Important Note

Ascending and descending orders contain exactly the same numbers, only in opposite directions.

**Skill:** Application

**Type:** Ordering (descending, using standard form)

**Question:**
Arrange the following in a descending order, using the standard form.
* Three milliard, forty million, seventy-one thousand, ten.
* (3 × 1,000,000,000) + (5 × 1,000,000) + (7 × 1,000) + (1 × 100) + (1 × 10)
* 3,000,000,000 + 30,000,000 + 10

**ANSWER:** 3,300,710,400 > 3,040,071,010 > 3,030,000,010 > 3,005,007,110

Step 3: Compare the Hundred Millions place: 3 (in 3,300,710,400), 0, 0, 0 → 3,300,710,400 is the greatest.

### Question 9 (Challenge)

**Skill:** Analysis

**Type:** Pattern / reasoning

**Question:**
The following numbers are arranged in a descending order:
3,751,924,096 → 3,751,924,069 → 3,751,624,096 → 3,751,624,069
If you replace each 6 by 9 and each 9 by 6, what do you notice?

**ANSWER:** After swapping the digits, the list becomes 3,751,624,069 → 3,751,624,096 → 3,751,924,069 → 3,751,924,096, which is now arranged in **ascending** order — the order is reversed.

**MODEL ANSWER:**
Step 1: Swap the digits in each number (every 6 becomes 9, every 9 becomes 6).
3,751,924,096 → 3,751,624,069
3,751,924,069 → 3,751,624,096
3,751,624,096 → 3,751,924,069
3,751,624,069 → 3,751,924,096
Step 2: Write the new list in the same positions: 3,751,624,069 ; 3,751,624,096 ; 3,751,924,069 ; 3,751,924,096.
Step 3: Compare neighbouring numbers: each one is less than the next, so the list is now in ascending order.
Step 4: Explain: swapping 6 and 9 turns every "bigger digit" into a "smaller digit" in the places that decided the order, so the descending order becomes ascending.

**Final Answer:** The order is reversed — the numbers are now in ascending order.

d = 588,310
Step 2: All have 6 digits; compare the Hundred Thousands place: 5 (d) < 6 (a, b, c).
Step 3: Compare a, b, c at the Ten Thousands place: 0 (c) < 4 (a) < 5 (b).
Step 4: Ascending: 588,310 < 604,302 < 645,301 < 653,310 → d, c, a, b.

## 9. Common Mistakes

### Common Mistake 1

**Mistake:** Mixing up the meanings of ascending order and descending order.

**Correct Method:** Ascending = least to greatest; descending = greatest to least.

**Why:** The source's "Remember" notes define both terms, and the note for parents asks to remind the child about the meaning of the two terms.

### Common Mistake 2

**Mistake:** Comparing numbers that are written in different forms without converting them.

**Correct Method:** Write every number in standard form first, as the source does in its table, then order them.

**Why:** Numbers in word, expanded or product form cannot be compared digit by digit until they are in standard form.

### Common Mistake 3

**Mistake:** Comparing digits that are not in the same place.

**Correct Method:** Align the numbers vertically so the places line up, then compare digits from left to right.

**Why:** The source's note for parents advises aligning the numbers vertically and comparing digits from left to right.

## 10. Lesson Summary

**Unit:** Unit 1 | **Lesson:** Lesson 7 | **Topic:** Descending and Ascending Numbers

## Key Concepts

* Ascending order is from least to greatest; descending order is from greatest to least.
* The number with the most digits is the greatest; the number with the fewest digits is the least.
* Numbers with the same number of digits are compared place by place from the left.
* Numbers given in word, expanded or product form must be written in standard form before ordering.

## Important Rules

* Count digits first, then compare from the greatest place value.
* Descending order is the reverse of ascending order for the same set.
* Line up places (write numbers under each other) before comparing.

## Important Formulas

* Ordering is repeated comparison: a > b > c means a is greatest and c is least.
* Example chain: 10,058,942 > 5,510,876 > 5,452,718 > 450,528.

## Key Vocabulary

* ascending order, descending order, order, standard form, greatest, least

## Problem-Solving Methods

* Three-step ascending method: find the smallest, compare the rest, write the list.
* Converting all numbers to standard form in a table before ordering.
* Using the number of digits as a quick first test.

## What Students Should Remember

* Ascending goes up, descending goes down.
* Always convert to standard form first and line up the places.
