## 1. Lesson Overview

**Unit:** Unit 1 | **Lesson:** Lesson 8 | **Topic:** Rounding whole numbers (midpoint strategy and place value strategy)

**Lesson title:** Rounding Rules

**Main mathematical topic:** Estimating a whole number by rounding it to the nearest ten, hundred, thousand, ten thousand, hundred thousand, million or milliard, using two different strategies.

**What students learn in Lesson 8:**

* When an exact number is not needed, you can **estimate** by **rounding** the number.
* Way 1 — the **midpoint strategy**: draw or imagine a number line between the two nearest round numbers, find the midpoint, and see which round number the number is closer to.
* Way 2 — the **place value strategy**: circle the digit in the rounding place, look at the digit to its right, then round up if that digit is 5 or greater and round down if it is less than 5; all digits to the right of the rounding place become zeros.
* The approximation symbol ≈ is read as "approximately equals".
* An error alert: when rounding a 9 up to the next greater digit, remember to regroup to the next place value (for example 999,999 ≈ 1,000,000).

**Main mathematical concepts:** estimate, rounding, midpoint, rounding place, rounding up, rounding down, approximately equals (≈).

**Important skills developed:** applying multiple strategies to round numbers; using a number line with a labelled midpoint; rounding to any named place; discussing whether an estimate is accurate.

## 3. Lesson Content

**Unit:** Unit 1 | **Lesson:** Lesson 8 | **Topic:** Rounding rules

### Mathematical Concepts

**Source situation:** A roller coaster is 2,181 meters long. About how long is the roller coaster? Since you do not need an exact number, you can **estimate** by **rounding** the number.

#### Way 1 — The midpoint strategy

Rounding 2,181 to the nearest thousand:

* 2,181 lies between 2,000 and 3,000; the midpoint of that interval is 2,500.
* 2,181 is closer to 2,000 than to 3,000.
* So, round 2,181 to 2,000.
* Written as `2,181 ≈ 2,000` and read as "2,181 approximately equals 2,000".

Number line description: 2,000 at the bottom, 2,500 marked as the midpoint, 3,000 at the top, and 2,181 placed just above 2,000.

#### Way 2 — The place value strategy

| Step 1 | Step 2 | Step 3 |
|---|---|---|
| Find the place you want to round to and circle the digit in that place. In 2,181 the Thousands digit 2 is circled. | Look at the digit to its right and underline it. In 2,181 the digit to the right is 1. | If the underlined digit is 5 or greater, round up. If the underlined digit is less than 5, round down. Change each digit to the right of the rounding place to 0. Since 1 is less than 5, 2,181 rounds to 2,000. |

Then, the roller coaster is about 2,000 meters long.

#### The Rounding Rule (source rhyme)

Circle the digit, look next door.
5 or higher? Add one more.
4 or less? Let it rest.

**Remember:** The digits to the right become zeroes.

#### Error alert (source)

When you round a 9 to the next greater digit, remember to regroup to the next place value.
Examples: 999,999 ≈ 1,000,000 (nearest ten thousand); 13,999,999 ≈ 14,000,000 (nearest hundred).

### Definitions

**Definition — Estimate**

An estimate is an approximate value used when an exact number is not needed.

**Definition — Rounding**

Rounding is replacing a number by the nearest number that ends in zeros at a chosen place, so the answer is easier to use.

**Definition — Midpoint**

The midpoint is the number exactly halfway between the two round numbers on either side of the number being rounded; for the nearest thousand between 2,000 and 3,000 the midpoint is 2,500.

**Definition — Approximately equals (≈)**

The symbol ≈ means "approximately equals"; 2,181 ≈ 2,000 is read as "2,181 approximately equals 2,000".

### Rules

**Rule — The rounding rule (place value strategy)**

Circle the digit in the rounding place and look at the digit to its right. If that digit is 5 or greater, add one to the rounding digit (round up). If that digit is less than 5, keep the rounding digit as it is (round down). Change every digit to the right of the rounding place to 0.

**Rule — The midpoint rule**

Compare the number with the midpoint of the interval between the two nearest round numbers: if the number is below the midpoint, round down; if it is at or above the midpoint, round up.

**Rule — Regrouping a 9**

When rounding up a 9, regroup to the next place value; for example 999,999 rounded to the nearest ten thousand is 1,000,000.

### Formulas

**Formula — Rounding decision**

`If (digit to the right of the rounding place) ≥ 5 → round up; if < 5 → round down`

**Where:**

* rounding place = the place named in the question (ten, hundred, thousand, ten thousand, hundred thousand, million, milliard)
* round up = add 1 to the digit in the rounding place, then write zeros to its right
* round down = keep the digit in the rounding place, then write zeros to its right

### Procedures / Methods

**Method: Midpoint strategy**

1. Identify the place you are rounding to.
2. Find the two round numbers just below and just above the given number at that place.
3. Find the midpoint of those two numbers.
4. Decide which round number the given number is closer to (compare with the midpoint).
5. Write the answer with the ≈ symbol.

**Method: Place value strategy**

1. Find and circle the digit in the rounding place.
2. Underline the digit immediately to its right.
3. If the underlined digit is 5 or greater, add 1 to the circled digit; if it is less than 5, leave the circled digit unchanged.
4. Change all digits to the right of the rounding place to 0.
5. Regroup if adding 1 to a 9 makes 10.

### Mathematical Relationships

* Both strategies always give the same answer; the midpoint strategy explains *why* the rule works.
* Rounding to a greater place (for example million rather than thousand) gives a rougher estimate.
* Rounding to the nearest thousand keeps every number from 311,500 to 312,499 as 312,000, which shows that many numbers round to the same result.

### Examples

#### Example 1 (source)

**Problem:** Use the midpoint strategy to round each of the following.
a. 74,231 (to the nearest 1,000) b. 9,360 (to the nearest 100)

**Solution:**
a. 74,231 is between 74,000 and 75,000; the midpoint is 74,500; 74,231 is below the midpoint, then 74,231 ≈ 74,000.
b. 9,360 is between 9,300 and 9,400; the midpoint is 9,350; 9,360 is above the midpoint, then 9,360 ≈ 9,400.

**Final Answer:** a. 74,000 b. 9,400

#### Example 2 (source)

**Problem:** Use the place value strategy to round each of the following.
a. 2,618 (to the nearest 10)
b. 174,568 (to the nearest 10,000)
c. 3,697,852,721 (to the nearest Ten Million)
d. 7,556,462 (to the nearest Million)
e. 999,999 (to the nearest Ten Thousand)
f. 13,999,999 (to the nearest Hundred)

**Solution (source reasoning):**
a. 8 > 5 → 2,618 ≈ 2,620
b. 4 < 5 → 174,568 ≈ 170,000
c. 7 > 5 → 3,697,852,721 ≈ 3,700,000,000
d. 5 = 5 → 7,556,462 ≈ 8,000,000
e. 9 > 5 → 999,999 ≈ 1,000,000
f. 9 > 5 → 13,999,999 ≈ 14,000,000

**Final Answer:** a. 2,620 b. 170,000 c. 3,700,000,000 d. 8,000,000 e. 1,000,000 f. 14,000,000

## 4. Key Mathematical Vocabulary

### Estimate

**Meaning:** An approximate value used when an exact number is not needed.
**Example:** The roller coaster is about 2,000 meters long.

### Rounding

**Meaning:** Replacing a number by the nearest number ending in zeros at a chosen place.
**Example:** 2,181 rounded to the nearest thousand is 2,000.

### Rounding place

**Meaning:** The place named in the question, whose digit may change when rounding.
**Example:** For "the nearest Thousand" in 2,181, the rounding place is the Thousands place.

### Midpoint

**Meaning:** The number exactly halfway between the two round numbers on either side.
**Example:** Between 9,300 and 9,400 the midpoint is 9,350.

### Round up / round down

**Meaning:** Round up = add 1 to the digit in the rounding place; round down = keep that digit unchanged. Either way, all digits to the right become zeros.
**Example:** 549 rounded to the nearest ten is 550 (round up); 423 is 420 (round down).

### Approximately equals (≈)

**Meaning:** The symbol showing that a value is a rounded, approximate value.
**Example:** 3,446 ≈ 3,000

## 5. Simplified Student Explanation

**Unit:** Unit 1 | **Lesson:** Lesson 8 | **Topic:** Rounding Rules

Sometimes we do not need the exact number. Instead we say "about". That is called **rounding**.

**The easy rule:**
1. Find the place you must round to and circle its digit.
2. Look at the digit just to the right (next door).
3. If that digit is 5 or more → add 1 to the circled digit.
4. If it is 4 or less → leave the circled digit alone.
5. Change every digit to the right into 0.

The book says it like a rhyme:
*Circle the digit, look next door. 5 or higher? Add one more. 4 or less? Let it rest.*

Example: round 2,181 to the nearest thousand.
* Circle the 2 (thousands). Next door is 1.
* 1 is less than 5, so the 2 stays.
* Zeros to the right → 2,000.
So 2,181 ≈ 2,000.

**The number line way (midpoint):**
2,181 is between 2,000 and 3,000. The middle is 2,500. Since 2,181 is below 2,500, it is closer to 2,000. Same answer.

**Watch out for 9s!** If you must add 1 to a 9, it becomes 10 and you regroup:
999,999 rounded to the nearest ten thousand becomes 1,000,000.

The symbol ≈ means "about" or "approximately equals".

## 6. Important Rules and Formulas

### Rule 1 — The Rounding Rule

Circle the digit in the rounding place, look at the digit next door (to the right): 5 or higher → add one more; 4 or less → let it rest.

### Rule 2 — Zeros to the right

All digits to the right of the rounding place become zeroes.

### Rule 3 — Midpoint rule

If the number is below the midpoint, round down; if it is at or above the midpoint, round up.

### Rule 4 — Regrouping 9s

When a 9 in the rounding place is rounded up, regroup into the next place value (999,999 ≈ 1,000,000).

### Formula 1 — Rounding decision

`digit to the right ≥ 5 → round up`
`digit to the right < 5 → round down`

### Important Note

The symbol ≈ is read "approximately equals"; a rounded answer is an estimate, not an exact value.

## 8. Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Midpoint strategy

**Problem:** Round 2,181 to the nearest thousand using the midpoint strategy.

**Method (textbook method):** Use a number line between the two nearest thousands and find the midpoint.

**Solution:**
Step 1: 2,181 lies between 2,000 and 3,000.
Step 2: The midpoint is 2,500.
Step 3: 2,181 is closer to 2,000 than to 3,000.
Step 4: Write 2,181 ≈ 2,000, read as "2,181 approximately equals 2,000".

**Final Answer:** 2,000 (the roller coaster is about 2,000 meters long)

### Example 2 — Place value strategy

**Problem:** Round 174,568 to the nearest 10,000.

**Method (textbook method):** Circle the digit in the rounding place, look at the digit to its right, apply the rounding rule.

**Solution:**
Step 1: The Ten Thousands digit is 7 (circle it).
Step 2: The digit to its right is 4 (underline it).
Step 3: 4 < 5, so let it rest — the 7 stays.
Step 4: Change all digits to the right of the Ten Thousands place to 0.

**Final Answer:** 170,000

### Example 3 — Rounding up a 9 (regrouping)

**Problem:** Round 999,999 to the nearest Ten Thousand.

**Method:** Apply the rounding rule and regroup when a 9 is rounded up.

**Solution:**
Step 1: The Ten Thousands digit is 9.
Step 2: The digit to its right (Thousands) is 9, and 9 ≥ 5, so add one more.
Step 3: Adding 1 to 99 ten thousands gives 100 ten thousands, which must be regrouped into the next place values.

**Final Answer:** 1,000,000

### Example 4 — Rounding exactly at 5

**Problem:** Round 7,556,462 to the nearest Million.

**Method:** Apply the rounding rule; a digit equal to 5 rounds up.

**Solution:**
Step 1: The Millions digit is 7.
Step 2: The digit to its right (Hundred Thousands) is 5, and 5 is "5 or higher".
Step 3: So 7 millions becomes 8 millions and all digits to the right become zeros.

**Final Answer:** 8,000,000

## 9. Common Mistakes

### Common Mistake 1

**Mistake:** Forgetting to regroup when a 9 is rounded up, for example writing 999,999 ≈ 1,000,000 as 1,00,000 or keeping a 9.

**Correct Method:** When you round a 9 to the next greater digit, regroup to the next place value: 999,999 ≈ 1,000,000.

**Why:** This is the source's ERROR ALERT: "When you round a 9 to the next greater digit, remember to regroup to the next place value."

### Common Mistake 2

**Mistake:** Rounding down when the digit to the right is exactly 5.

**Correct Method:** A digit of 5 rounds up ("5 or higher? Add one more"), so 7,556,462 ≈ 8,000,000 to the nearest million.

**Why:** The source's rounding rule and the parents' note both state that you round up if the digit to the right of the rounding place is equal to or greater than 5.

### Common Mistake 3

**Mistake:** Leaving the original digits to the right of the rounding place instead of writing zeros, for example writing 2,357 ≈ 2,367.

**Correct Method:** Change each digit to the right of the rounding place to 0 → 2,357 ≈ 2,360.

**Why:** The source's "Remember" note says the digits to the right become zeroes.

### Common Mistake 4

**Mistake:** Trying to round a number that is written in words or in expanded form without writing it in standard form first.

**Correct Method:** Write the standard form of the number before rounding.

**Why:** The source's note for parents says, if necessary, to allow the child to write the standard form of the number before rounding.

## 10. Lesson Summary

**Unit:** Unit 1 | **Lesson:** Lesson 8 | **Topic:** Rounding Rules

## Key Concepts

* When an exact number is not needed, estimate by rounding.
* Two strategies: the midpoint strategy (number line) and the place value strategy (rounding rule).
* The symbol ≈ means "approximately equals".
* Digits to the right of the rounding place become zeros.
* Rounding a 9 up requires regrouping to the next place value.

## Important Rules

* Circle the digit in the rounding place, look next door: 5 or higher → add one more; 4 or less → let it rest.
* Midpoint rule: below the midpoint → round down; at or above the midpoint → round up.
* Regroup when adding 1 to a 9.

## Important Formulas

* `digit to the right ≥ 5 → round up`; `digit to the right < 5 → round down`.
* Midpoints: between 2,000 and 3,000 → 2,500; between 9,300 and 9,400 → 9,350; between 36,900 and 37,000 → 36,950.

## Key Vocabulary

* estimate, rounding, rounding place, midpoint, round up, round down, approximately equals (≈)

## Problem-Solving Methods

* Drawing a number line with the midpoint labelled to decide the rounding.
* Applying the rounding rule digit by digit.
* Identifying the rounding place from a rounded result (5,900,000 → Hundred Thousand).
* Finding the range of numbers that round to a given result (311,500 to 312,499 all round to 312,000).

## What Students Should Remember

* A rounded answer is an estimate, written with ≈.
* 5 always rounds up.
* All digits to the right of the rounding place become 0.
