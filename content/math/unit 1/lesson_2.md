## 3. Lesson Content

**Unit:** Unit 1 | **Lesson:** Lesson 2 | **Topic:** Changing place values

### Mathematical Concepts

#### Concept 1 — A base-ten (tens) place-value system

* The value of a digit changes as it moves to the left within a number.
* Our place-value system is based on **tens**.
* Each place value in this system is **10 times** the one to the right of it.

**Source table — the digit 2 in different places (each step to the left is × 10):**

| Place | Millions | Hundred Thousands | Ten Thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Digit | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Value | 2,000,000 | 200,000 | 20,000 | 2,000 | 200 | 20 | 2 |

Reading the table from right to left, each value is 10 times the value before it: 2 → 20 → 200 → 2,000 → 20,000 → 200,000 → 2,000,000.

#### Concept 2 — A digit is 10 times the same digit one place to its right

A digit in one place equals 10 times the digit in the place to its right.

**Source example:** The Hundreds place is 10 times the Tens place, so the value of 2 changes from 20 to 200.

#### Concept 3 — The pattern of zeros

Observe the pattern in the number of zeros: each move one place to the left adds one zero to the value of the digit (2, 20, 200, 2,000, …).

### Definitions

**Definition — Base-ten place-value system**

A number system in which each place value is 10 times the place value immediately to its right, so the places are Ones, Tens, Hundreds, Thousands, Ten Thousands, Hundred Thousands, Millions, and so on.

### Rules

**Rule — The 10 times rule**

Each place value is 10 times the place value to its right. Therefore a digit in one place equals 10 times the same digit in the place to its right.

**Rule — Moving one place to the left**

If a digit moves one space to the left on the place-value chart, its value becomes 10 times as great (one extra zero).

**Rule — Moving more than one place to the left**

Moving two places to the left multiplies the value by 100; moving three places to the left multiplies the value by 1,000.

### Formulas

**Formula — Value after moving left**

`New value = old value × 10^n`

**Where:**

* old value = the value of the digit in its original place
* n = the number of places the digit moves to the left
* For n = 1 the value is 10 times greater; for n = 2 it is 100 times greater; for n = 3 it is 1,000 times greater.

**Formula — Renaming with place-value units**

`Number = (how many units) × (value of the unit)`

**Where:**

* unit = tens, hundreds, thousands, millions, …
* Example: 60 tens = 60 × 10 = 600; 750 hundreds = 750 × 100 = 75,000 = 75 thousands.

### Procedures / Methods

**Method: Finding how many times greater one digit's value is than another**

1. Find the value of the digit in the first number.
2. Find the value of the same digit in the second number.
3. Divide the greater value by the smaller value, or count how many places apart they are.
4. Each place apart = 10 times; two places = 100 times; three places = 1,000 times.

**Method: Renaming a number using a place-value unit**

1. Decide which unit is asked for (tens, hundreds, thousands, millions).
2. Write the value of that unit (ten = 10, hundred = 100, thousand = 1,000, million = 1,000,000).
3. Count how many of those units make the number (remove the matching zeros, or divide by the value of the unit).

**Source examples:** 23,800 = 238 hundreds; 60 tens = 600; 750 hundreds = 75 thousands (because 750 × 100 = 75,000).

### Mathematical Relationships

* 10 ones = 1 ten; 10 tens = 1 hundred; 10 hundreds = 1 thousand; 10 thousands = 1 ten thousand; 10 ten thousands = 1 hundred thousand; 10 hundred thousands = 1 million.
* Hundred thousand is 10 times ten thousand.
* One million is 10 times one hundred thousand.
* 7,000 thousands = 7 millions (because 7,000 × 1,000 = 7,000,000).

### Examples

## 4. Key Mathematical Vocabulary

### Tens (base-ten) system

**Meaning:** The place-value system used for whole numbers, in which every place is 10 times the place to its right.
**Example:** The Hundreds place is 10 times the Tens place.

### 10 times

**Meaning:** Multiplied by 10; the relationship between a place and the place immediately to its right.
**Example:** 200 is 10 times 20.

### Moving to the left

**Meaning:** Putting a digit in a higher place on the place-value chart, which makes its value 10 times greater for each place moved.
**Example:** The digit 5 moving from the Hundreds place to the Thousands place changes value from 500 to 5,000.

### Renaming (using place-value units)

**Meaning:** Writing the same number using a different unit, such as tens, hundreds or thousands.
**Example:** 23,800 = 238 hundreds.

## 5. Simplified Student Explanation

**Unit:** Unit 1 | **Lesson:** Lesson 2 | **Topic:** Changing Values

Our number system works in tens. That means every place is 10 times bigger than the place on its right.

Look at the digit 2 moving to the left:

2 → 20 → 200 → 2,000 → 20,000 → 200,000 → 2,000,000

Each time the 2 moves one place to the left, it gets 10 times bigger and gains one more zero.

So:
* One place to the left → 10 times bigger
* Two places to the left → 100 times bigger
* Three places to the left → 1,000 times bigger

Example: the 7 in the Thousands place is worth 7,000 and the 7 in the Tens place is worth 70. How many times bigger? 7,000 ÷ 70 = 100 times. (They are two places apart.)

You can also rename numbers using units:
* 60 tens = 60 × 10 = 600
* 56,000 = 56 thousands
* 750 hundreds = 75,000 = 75 thousands

Quick way: to change tens into a number, add one zero. To change hundreds into a number, add two zeros. To change thousands into a number, add three zeros.

## 6. Important Rules and Formulas

### Rule 1 — 10 times rule

Each place value is 10 times the place to its right, so a digit is worth 10 times more each time it moves one place to the left.

### Rule 2 — Counting places

Two places to the left → 100 times greater. Three places to the left → 1,000 times greater.

### Formula 1 — Renaming

`Number = (number of units) × (value of the unit)`
Example: 30 tens = 30 × 10 = 300.

### Formula 2 — Useful equivalences

* 10 ten thousands = 1 hundred thousand
* 10 hundred thousands = 1 million
* 1,000 thousands = 1 million
* 7,000 thousands = 7 millions

### Important Note

Watch the zeros: each step to the left adds exactly one zero to the value of the digit.

### Question 1

**Skill:** Understanding

**Type:** Explanation

**Question:**
How does the value of 5 change as it moves from the Hundreds place to the Thousands place?

**ANSWER:** Its value becomes 10 times greater: it changes from 500 to 5,000.

**MODEL ANSWER:**
Step 1: In the Hundreds place the digit 5 has the value 500.
Step 2: The Thousands place is one place to the left of the Hundreds place.
Step 3: Each place is 10 times the place to its right, so 500 × 10 = 5,000.

**Final Answer:** The value changes from 500 to 5,000, which is 10 times greater.

### Question 4 (Challenge)

**Skill:** Problem Solving

**Type:** Forming numbers and analysing a changing value

**Question:**
Use the digits 5, 7, 3, 1, 8, 2, 9 and 6 to make the greatest number you can, then use the same digits to make the smallest number you can.
* The greatest is ______
* The smallest is ______
* How did the value of 7 change from the greatest number to the smallest number? Why did it change? Use words and numbers to explain your thinking.

**ANSWER:**
The greatest number is 98,765,321. The smallest number is 12,356,789.
In 98,765,321 the digit 7 is in the Hundred Thousands place, so its value is 700,000. In 12,356,789 the digit 7 is in the Hundreds place, so its value is 700. The value became 1,000 times smaller because the digit moved three places to the right.

**MODEL ANSWER:**
Step 1: To make the greatest number, arrange the digits from greatest to least: 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 3, 2, 1 → 98,765,321.
Step 2: To make the smallest number, arrange the digits from least to greatest: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 → 12,356,789.
Step 3: Find the value of 7 in each number.
In 98,765,321 the periods are 98 | 765 | 321, so the 7 is in the Hundred Thousands place → 700,000.
In 12,356,789 the periods are 12 | 356 | 789, so the 7 is in the Hundreds place → 700.
Step 4: Compare the two values: 700,000 ÷ 700 = 1,000.
Step 5: Explain: each place is 10 times the place to its right, and the 7 moved three places to the right, so its value was divided by 10 three times (10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000).

**Final Answer:** Greatest 98,765,321; smallest 12,356,789; the value of 7 changed from 700,000 to 700, which is 1,000 times smaller, because its place changed.

## 8. Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Comparing the value of the same digit in two numbers

**Problem:** The value of the digit 3 in 7,431,210 equals how many times the value of the digit 3 in 4,563,809?

**Method (textbook method):** Find the value of the digit in each number, then compare using the 10 times rule.

**Solution:**
Step 1: In 7,431,210 the periods are 7 | 431 | 210, so the 3 is in the Ten Thousands place → value 30,000.
Step 2: In 4,563,809 the periods are 4 | 563 | 809, so the 3 is in the Thousands place → value 3,000.
Step 3: 30,000 ÷ 3,000 = 10.

**Final Answer:** 10 times

### Example 2 — Thousands place compared with Tens place

**Problem:** The value of 7 in the Thousands place = how many times the value of 7 in the Tens place?

**Method:** Write both values and divide.

**Solution:**
Step 1: 7 in the Thousands place = 7,000.
Step 2: 7 in the Tens place = 70.
Step 3: 7,000 ÷ 70 = 100.

**Final Answer:** 100 times

### Example 3 — Renaming thousands as millions

**Problem:** 7,000 thousands = ______ millions.

**Method (textbook "think" hint):** Write the number in standard form first.

**Solution:**
Step 1: 7,000 thousands = 7,000 × 1,000 = 7,000,000.
Step 2: 7,000,000 = 7 millions.

**Final Answer:** 7 millions

## 9. Common Mistakes

### Common Mistake 1

**Mistake:** Saying that a digit moved one place to the left is 100 times greater (or writing two extra zeros).

**Correct Method:** One place to the left = 10 times greater = one extra zero; two places = 100 times.

**Why:** The source states that each place value in this system is 10 times the one to the right of it, and asks students to observe the pattern in the number of zeros.

### Common Mistake 2

**Mistake:** Renaming incorrectly, for example writing 30 tens = 30 instead of 300.

**Correct Method:** Multiply the number of units by the value of the unit: 30 tens = 30 × 10 = 300, as the source's own "think" hint shows.

**Why:** A "ten" is a unit worth 10, so 30 of them are worth 300.
