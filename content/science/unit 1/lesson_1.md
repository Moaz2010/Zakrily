## 1. Lesson Overview

Lesson 1, "Let's Find Living Organisms," introduces students to the difference between living organisms and non-living things, the three main characteristics that define living organisms (feeding, growth, breathing), and the concept of a habitat — the place where a living organism lives. Students learn why different living organisms are found in different places, and they practice a three-step method (Guess, Search, Record) for exploring the habitats of living organisms around them. Main scientific concepts covered: living organisms vs. non-living things, characteristics of living organisms, habitat, and the relationship between living organisms and their environment. Key skill developed: identifying and reasoning about where living organisms are likely to be found based on their needs.

---

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of Lesson 1, students should be able to:

* Distinguish between living organisms and nonliving things.
* Explore where living organisms are found.
* Identify the characteristics that make something a living organism (feeding, growth, breathing).
* Explain what a habitat is and give examples.
* Describe the three steps used to explore the habitats of living organisms (Guess, Search, Record).

## 3. Lesson Content

**Unit:** Chapter 1 | **Lesson:** Lesson 1 | **Topic:** Living Organisms, Non-Living Things, and Habitats

### Concepts

**Living organisms and non-living things:** Nature around us contains both living organisms and non-living things.
* Examples of living organisms: humans, animals, plants.
* Examples of non-living things: air, water, rocks.

Living organisms differ from non-living things because living organisms have several characteristics, including feeding, growth, and breathing.

### Definitions

**Definition — Living organisms**
Everything that can feed, grow, and breathe, such as humans, animals, and plants.

**Definition — Habitat**
The place where a living organism lives.

**Definition — Environment**
Everything around a living organism, including the other living organisms and non-living things around it. (Note: this appears in Lesson 1 as a "for illustration only" note, and it is expanded further in Lesson 4.)

### Scientific Facts

* The kinds of living organisms differ from one habitat to another, because living organisms live in habitats that meet their needs, such as water, food, and shelter.
* Living organisms cannot all live in the same place — each living organism looks for a habitat that suits its needs.
* Living organisms may be found near grass or trees, or under stones, in the shade of rocks, or under the ground.

### Habitats of Living Organisms — Where Living Organisms May Be Found

**1. Near grass or trees**
* Reason: these are places with food, and places that are easy to hide in.
* Examples: sparrows (food = insects/seeds on the tree; hiding place = grass), grasshoppers.

**2. Under stones, in the shade of rocks, or under the ground**
* Reason: these are damp places that contain water, and cool places away from the Sun.
* Example: ants (their nests are shown as cool, away from the Sun, and damp, containing water).

### Living Organisms and the Environment

Living organisms live in ways that suit the environment they live in. For example, some living organisms can live in hot, dry places such as deserts by living:
* In the shade of rocks, **or**
* Under the ground

**Cause and Effect**
* **Cause:** The desert Sun is intense during the day.
* **Effect:** Living organisms live under the ground or in the shade of rocks, to avoid the intense heat of the Sun in the desert during the day.

### Process — Steps for Exploring the Habitats of Living Organisms (Activity: "Exploring the Habitats of Some Living Organisms")

1. **Guess:** Predict places where living organisms might be found, such as flower beds, roots of big trees, gaps between rocks.
2. **Search:** Go outside and look for living organisms in the places you predicted. If you find one, watch it quietly and carefully.
3. **Record:** When you find a living organism, record both: the name of the living organism, and the kind of place where you found it.

### Examples — Habitats of Specific Living Organisms (from the Activity table)

| Living organism | Habitat (Place) |
|---|---|
| Sparrow | On trees |
| Spider | Flower beds; gaps between rocks |
| Lizard | Under the shade of rocks; under stones; on hot rocks |
| Scorpion | Under stones; gaps between rocks |
| Dung beetle | Deserts; farmland near the Nile River |

### Important Notes

* **Caution:** When searching for living organisms, do not touch them unless your teacher or science guide helps you.
* All living organisms cannot live in the same place — this is why the kinds of living organisms found differ from one location to another.

## 5. Simplified Student Explanation

Look around you — nature is full of two kinds of things:

* **Living organisms**, like humans, animals, and plants.
* **Non-living things**, like air, water, and rocks.

How do we know something is a living organism? A living organism can do three things:
* It can **feed** (eat).
* It can **grow** (get bigger).
* It can **breathe**.

Non-living things like rocks and water cannot do these things.

Every living organism needs a home — a place to live. We call this place a **habitat**. Not every living organism lives in the same habitat. Each one looks for a place that has what it needs: food, water, and a safe place to hide.

* Sparrows and grasshoppers like to live **near grass or trees**, because there is food there and it's easy to hide.
* Ants like to live **under stones or underground**, because it is cool and a little damp there.
* In the hot desert, animals often live **under rocks or underground** so they can escape the strong heat of the Sun.

If you want to find living organisms outside, you can follow three simple steps:
1. **Guess** where you might find one.
2. **Search** for it in that place.
3. **Record** what you found and where you found it.

Remember: never touch a living organism you find outside unless your teacher says it's okay!

## 7. Worked Examples / Scientific Reasoning

### Example 1

**Problem:**

## 8. Lesson Summary

### Key Ideas

* Nature contains living organisms (humans, animals, plants) and non-living things (air, water, rocks).
* Living organisms differ from non-living things because living organisms feed, grow, and breathe.
* A habitat is the place where a living organism lives.
* Living organisms live in habitats that meet their needs (food, water, shelter), so different living organisms are found in different places.

### Key Facts

* Near grass or trees: places with food and easy hiding — home to sparrows and grasshoppers.
* Under stones, in the shade of rocks, or underground: damp and cool places — home to ants.
* In hot, dry places (deserts), living organisms live in the shade of rocks or underground to avoid the Sun's intense heat.

### Key Vocabulary

* Living organisms — everything that can feed, grow, and breathe.
* Non-living things — things that cannot feed, grow, or breathe (air, water, rocks).
* Habitat — the place where a living organism lives.
* Environment — everything around a living organism, including other living organisms and non-living things.

### Important Relationships

* **Cause:** Habitats differ in what they offer (food, water, shelter, shade). **Effect:** Different kinds of living organisms are found in different habitats.
* **Cause:** The desert Sun is intense during the day. **Effect:** Living organisms shelter under the ground or in the shade of rocks.

### What Students Should Remember

* Living organisms = feed + grow + breathe.
* Habitat = the place a living organism lives.
* To explore habitats: Guess → Search → Record.
* Never touch a living organism you find outdoors without a teacher's help.
