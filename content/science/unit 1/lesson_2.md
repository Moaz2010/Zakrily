## 1. Lesson Overview

Lesson 2, "How to Observe," teaches students how to carefully observe living organisms and how to record what they observe. Students learn the three features to look at when observing a living organism (shape, color, size), how to correctly use a magnifying glass depending on whether the object can be moved or not, and how to prepare a proper record card with a sketch and descriptive sentences. Main scientific concepts covered: observation, magnifying glass technique, and scientific recording. Key skill developed: using a tool (magnifying glass) correctly and documenting observations in an organized, structured way (a record card).

---

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of Lesson 2, students should be able to:

* Use a magnifying glass to observe details of some living organisms.
* Correctly prepare a record card for some living organisms.
* Identify the correct way to hold and move a magnifying glass depending on whether the observed object can be moved.
* Explain the safety precautions to follow when observing living organisms with a magnifying glass.

## 3. Lesson Content

**Unit:** Chapter 1 | **Lesson:** Lesson 2 | **Topic:** Observing Living Organisms with a Magnifying Glass and Recording Observations

### Concepts

**Rules for observation:** When looking closely at living organisms, you must observe three features: Shape, Color, and Size. To see fine details of some living organisms, a magnifying glass is used, and then a record card is written.

### Definitions

**Definition — Magnifying glass**
A tool used to help see details of some living organisms (such as insects and plants).

**Definition — Record card**
A written and sketched record of an observation of a living organism, including its topic, date, grade, name, a sketch, and descriptive sentences about its size, color, shape, and location.

### Scientific Facts

* A magnifying glass can be used in one of two ways, depending on whether the observed object can be moved.
* A record card should always start with the topic, then the date/grade/name, then the recorded observation (sketch + descriptive sentences), and finally what was learned or any remaining questions.
* A good scientific sketch uses thin, clear lines with no shading.

### Processes / Steps

**First — How to Use a Magnifying Glass** (two situations)

1. **When the object can be moved** (such as a small leaf): Hold the magnifying glass close to your eye, then move the object back and front to get a clear image.
2. **When the object cannot be moved** (such as a tree trunk): Hold the magnifying glass close to your eye, then move your face together with the magnifying glass back and front to get a clear image.

**Second — How to Write a Record Card** (four steps)

1. Write the topic.
2. Write the date, grade, and your name.
3. Record what you found using: (a) a sketch with thin, clear lines and no shading, and (b) sentences to describe the characteristics of the living organism you observed (its size, color, and shape), and the place (location) where you found it.
4. Write what you learned, or write the questions you are thinking about.

### Examples

**Activity: Observing a Living Organism Closely — Dung beetle and Plant leaf**

* Dung beetle: Look at the insect's shape and body parts, such as small hairs and leg joints.
* Plant leaf: Look at the leaf and observe the lines (veins) on it.

**Example record card — Dung beetle**

| Field | Observation |
|---|---|
| Size | About 2 cm |
| Color | Black (Blackish) |
| Shape | Oval-shaped with long back legs |
| Location | On the ground near the Nile River |
| Notes | It rolled a perfectly round ball of dung backward while standing on its head. |

**Example record card — Plant leaf**

| Field | Observation |
|---|---|
| Size | About 6 cm |
| Color | Dark green |
| Shape | Oval |
| Location | Tree branches |
| Notes | A prominent long vein is found at the center of the leaf, with a network of smaller veins branching from it on both sides. |

### Important Notes

* **Caution:** Never look directly at the Sun with a magnifying glass, because it can harm your eyes.
* **Caution:** Wear gloves while observing a living organism, to protect your hands from getting polluted.

## 5. Simplified Student Explanation

When you want to learn about a living organism up close, you need to observe three things about it: its **shape**, its **color**, and its **size**.

To see very small details — like the tiny hairs on an insect's leg — you can use a **magnifying glass**. There are two ways to use it:

* If the object can move (like a leaf), hold the glass close to your eye and move the **leaf** back and forth until it looks clear.
* If the object cannot move (like a tree trunk), hold the glass close to your eye and move **your face** (with the glass) back and forth until it looks clear.

**Important safety rules:**
* Never point the magnifying glass at the Sun — it can hurt your eyes!
* Wear gloves so your hands stay clean and protected.

After observing, scientists write down what they saw on a **record card**. A record card has four parts:
1. The **topic** (what you are observing).
2. The **date, grade, and your name**.
3. A **sketch** (a simple drawing with clear lines, no shading) plus **sentences** describing the size, color, shape, and location.
4. What you **learned**, or questions you still have.

For example, a dung beetle's record card might say: "Size: about 2 cm, Color: black, Shape: oval with long back legs, Location: near the Nile River."

## 7. Worked Examples / Scientific Reasoning

### Example 1

**Problem:**
A student wants to observe a plant leaf on a tree branch closely. The leaf can be gently held and moved, but the tree itself cannot be moved. Explain how the student should use the magnifying glass to see the leaf's veins clearly.

**Solution:**
Step 1: Since the leaf itself can be moved (even though it grows on a tree), treat it as a movable object.
Step 2: Hold the magnifying glass close to your eye.
Step 3: Move the leaf back and forth in front of the magnifying glass until the veins come into clear focus.

**Final Answer:**
The student should hold the magnifying glass close to the eye and move the leaf back and forth (not the face) until the leaf's veins are seen clearly, since the leaf itself is the object that can be moved.

## 8. Lesson Summary

### Key Ideas

* When observing living organisms closely, look at shape, color, and size.
* A magnifying glass helps see fine details of small living organisms like insects and plants.
* A record card is used to write down and sketch observations of a living organism.

### Key Facts

* If the object can be moved (like a leaf): hold the magnifying glass close to your eye, and move the object back and front.
* If the object cannot be moved (like a tree trunk): hold the magnifying glass close to your eye, and move your face with the glass back and front.
* A record card should include: topic; date/grade/name; a sketch (thin, clear lines, no shading) plus descriptive sentences (size, color, shape, location); and what was learned or remaining questions.

### Key Vocabulary

* Magnifying glass — a tool used to help see details of some living organisms.
* Record card — a card used to sketch and describe observations of a living organism.
* Sketch — a simple drawing with thin, clear lines and no shading.

### Important Relationships

* **Cause:** The object being observed cannot move (e.g., a tree trunk). **Effect:** The observer must move their face (with the glass), not the object.
* **Cause:** Looking directly at the Sun through a magnifying glass. **Effect:** This can harm your eyes — so it must never be done.

### What Students Should Remember

* Observe shape, color, and size when examining a living organism.
* Use the correct magnifying glass technique depending on whether the object can move.
* Never point a magnifying glass at the Sun; always wear gloves while observing.
* A record card needs a topic, date/grade/name, a sketch, descriptive sentences, and what you learned.
